The guide

Penalty rates, casual loading, and what to do if the numbers don't match.

Penalty rates

Awards pay more for work at times most people are off: weekends, public holidays, late nights and early mornings. Each award sets its own rates and its own time windows. A Sunday in fast food is not paid the same as a Sunday in retail or hospitality.

That's why this checker lists the rates straight from your award, and lets you choose which one applies to each shift.

Casual loading

Casual employees don't get paid leave, so most awards add a loading to the base hourly rate, usually 25%. Casual penalty rates in most awards already include it.

The casual rates in this checker come from the award data and already include the loading your award sets.

If you think you're underpaid

Keep your payslips, rosters and any record of the hours you worked. Ask your employer, in writing, which award and classification they pay you under and how your pay was worked out.

If it isn't fixed, the Fair Work Ombudsman can help for free. You can generally claim unpaid wages for up to six years.

Questions

Common questions about award pay.

How do I know which award covers me?

Your award depends on your employer's industry and the work you do. Search for your job or industry in the checker. If you're unsure, the Fair Work Ombudsman's Find my award tool walks you through it. Some workers are covered by an enterprise agreement instead of an award.

What are penalty rates?

Penalty rates are higher hourly rates an award sets for work at certain times, such as weekends, public holidays, late nights and early mornings. Each award sets its own rates and times, so a Sunday rate in fast food is different from a Sunday rate in retail or hospitality.

What is casual loading?

Casual employees don't get paid leave, so most awards pay them a loading on top of the base hourly rate, usually 25%. The casual rates in this checker already include the loading set by your award.

Is this checker's result legal advice?

No. It's an estimate based on the minimum rates in the Fair Work Commission's Modern Awards Pay Database. It doesn't cover enterprise agreements, allowances, overtime rules that depend on your roster, or individual arrangements. For a dispute, contact the Fair Work Ombudsman.

What should I do if I think I'm underpaid?

Check your payslips and timesheets, then raise it with your employer in writing and ask them to explain the rate. If it isn't fixed, the Fair Work Ombudsman can help for free. Generally you can recover unpaid wages from up to six years ago.
